Years. Its backbone.

Born and raised on Molokaʻi, where her ʻohana lived close to the land and the ocean — hunting, fishing, gathering, and the practices passed down through family and community. Culture wasn't something studied. It was lived.

Danced hula from five. At nine, the big stage: the Maui Hula Bowl — traveling between Molokaʻi and Maui with Ka ʻOhana Aliʻi Productions and Pueo Pata. Boarding at Lahainaluna, in a town where history, land, people, and culture were one thing.

Then Old Lahaina Luau: first as a hostess, then as one of the dancers on its stage — for fourteen years. Then a decade producing and performing private events across the island, and event planning and coordination on top of that. Both sides of the stage.

Twenty-five years. She isn't new to this industry. She has been its backbone for a long time.

"As a young girl from Molokaʻi, I had once sat in the audience dreaming about dancing on that very stage."
Now — same heart, same intention. Just bigger stages and more intentional experiences.

The show

Four movements.

The best show on Maui, bar none — and here is what that means.

01
Hula Kahiko

The pahu. Chant, percussion, the strength of kāne and wahine together. Jess's earliest lasting impression was John Kaimikaua's kāne at Ka Hula Piko on Molokaʻi — the drum reaching past what you could see. That is the feeling this movement is built to give a room.

02
Hula ʻAuana

The islands through mele — melody, story, movement, implements. Live musicians who bring their own connection to each song, so the music is part of the storytelling, not behind it.

03
Tahitian

Dancers nurtured inside the practice, from the school of Jess's close friend Tereva — rooted in Tahiti, teaching her culture here on Maui, inspiring many through her passion for the performing arts. Not a segment of a Polynesian show — a culture carried by people who grew up in it.

04
Fire
Tahitian fire and fire knife, woven into the Tahitian movement so the energy builds instead of waiting for the end.

The cast loves it, and the room can tell.

Fire knife — siva afi — is Samoan in origin. We credit each culture in every program.

What makes it different

The people behind it.

Our dancers come from hālau backgrounds. They aren't entertainers learning choreography for a stage — they've been surrounded by their cultures from a young age: the histories, the stories, the family teachings. That's what a guest feels, even when they can't name it.

And it runs the other way, too. Tereva trains the young dancers of Maui — into professionals. When they're ready, they carry straight over into Ulana. A girl who starts in her classes at eight has a career in her own cultural practice waiting for her.

How we'll do it

Done right.

The next time there's a show in the Gardens, no one should be able to say it wasn't done right.

We're building Ulana to hotel-vendor standards:

General liability coverage, the venue named as additional insured, certificates on your timeline
Fire Department approval for every fire segment
A written contract with every performer — nobody on stage without one
Sound levels and end times agreed in advance and kept, every show
One point of contact for you, and a show file for every night — who, when, what, and what changed

We say "building" on purpose. We'll show you each piece as it's in hand — not before.
